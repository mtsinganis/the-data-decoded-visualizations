China's rapid industrialization increased demand for Australian resources, especially iron ore and coal, helping fuel Australia's mining boom. The [Reserve Bank of Australia](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2011/2011-07/dev-implications-australia.html) documents that trade relationship. Yet their goods-export complexity rankings moved in opposite directions from 1995 to 2024: China rose from 42nd to 10th, while Australia moved from 33rd to 73rd. The main chart highlights those two trajectories; the other 143 economies provide grey context.

The second chart shows two more Asian economies that overtook Australia in the rankings. Vietnam rose from 119th to 44th, and the Philippines from 69th to 38th; 142 other economies, including China, appear in grey. China passed Australia between the 1997 and 1998 observations. The Philippines passed between 2009 and 2010. Vietnam first passed between 2013 and 2014, fell behind again the following year, and moved ahead once more between 2015 and 2016. These rankings describe the export mix; the charts do not establish that the resource boom caused Australia's ranking to fall.

The index reflects the diversity and sophistication of a country's goods exports, not its wealth or overall economic performance. Each chart retains only the annotations for its highlighted countries; together, the four annotations describe broad developments associated with the trajectories. Their visual positions do not identify particular years, events, or causes.

## Sources and methodology

Use the official [Harvard Growth Lab Growth Projections and Complexity Rankings dataset](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/XTAQMC), version 12.0, released February 8, 2026 and accessed October 12, 2026. Every year uses the same HS92 classification and historical vintage. Original global ranks are preserved. All 4,319 ranked country-year values match the [Nations Inquiry compilation](https://nationsinquiry.com/datasets/economic-complexity/), which provides display names. There is no background sampling or recalculation of ranks.

Each highlighted country has 30 annual observations. Ranked coverage is 140 economies in 1995–1999, 144 in 2000–2005, and 145 from 2006 onward. Economies are drawn only where observations exist; missing ranks are not filled and series are split at gaps. Curves connect annual observations, not independently measured continuous rankings. Rounded scores can tie; the supplied published ranks are unique. The Dataverse descriptive coverage field ends at 2023, while the actual version 12.0 CSV includes 2024.

For the resource-boom context, see the Reserve Bank of Australia’s [analysis of China’s industrialisation and Australian resource demand](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2011/2011-07/dev-implications-australia.html). The contextual annotations draw on the World Bank's [Viet Nam 2045: Trading Up in a Changing World](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/vietnam/publication/viet-nam-2045-trading-up-in-a-changing-world) and [Viet Nam export-diversification discussion](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099032124063537994/pdf/P1770151f0870f0a21bff71b52c2fd5976f.pdf), Harvard Kennedy School's [discussion of increasingly sophisticated Chinese manufacturing](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/china-us-faces-challenges-achieving-inclusive-growth-through-manufacturing), and Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade [2024 leading exports table](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/australias-goods-and-services-by-top-25-exports-2024.pdf.pdf). These are context, not a decomposition or causal explanation of ECI changes.

Ranks and normalized scores are relative. Coverage, classification concordances, reporting differences, revisions, gross export composition, and inclusion thresholds can affect them. Export sophistication does not directly measure domestic value added or know-how in every part of the economy; assembling sophisticated products can rely on imported inputs. Australia's commodity specialization does not establish generalized economic deterioration. See Harvard's [trade-data methodology](https://atlas.hks.harvard.edu/trade-data-methodology/). No causal effect can be inferred from these trajectories alone.

The Philippines contextual annotation is supported by the Department of Trade and Industry's [discussion of its semiconductor and electronics export specialization](https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-news-archived/dti-dti-chief-pascual-encourages-us-companies-invest-semiconductor-industry). It describes export composition rather than the domestic value added of every stage of semiconductor production.
