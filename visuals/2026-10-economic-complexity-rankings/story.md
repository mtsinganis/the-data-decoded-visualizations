Vietnam rose from 119th to 44th in global Economic Complexity Index rankings between 1995 and 2024. China climbed from 42nd to 10th. The Philippines rose from 69th to 38th. Australia moved from 33rd to 73rd. The chart includes all 145 economies with available observations; Vietnam, China, the Philippines, and Australia are highlighted, and the other 141 are grey. Vietnam uses purple, China green, and the Philippines orange, and Australia brand blue.

China passed Australia between the 1997 and 1998 observations. Vietnam first passed Australia between 2013 and 2014, fell behind again between 2014 and 2015, and moved ahead once more between 2015 and 2016. The title describes the long-term outcome; it does not imply uninterrupted rises or declines. The Philippines passed Australia between 2009 and 2010, rising from 56th to 51st while Australia moved from 55th to 59th. The underlying scores support their directions: Vietnam -0.968 to 0.672, China 0.628 to 1.271, and Australia 0.768 to 0.131, and the Philippines 0.128 to 0.739.

The index reflects the diversity and sophistication of a country's goods exports, not its wealth or overall economic performance. The four annotations describe broad developments associated with the trajectories. Their visual positions do not identify particular years, events, or causes.

## Sources and methodology

Use the official [Harvard Growth Lab Growth Projections and Complexity Rankings dataset](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/XTAQMC), version 12.0, released February 8, 2026 and accessed October 12, 2026. Every year uses the same HS92 classification and historical vintage. Original global ranks are preserved. All 4,319 ranked country-year values match the [Nations Inquiry compilation](https://nationsinquiry.com/datasets/economic-complexity/), which provides display names. There is no background sampling or recalculation of ranks.

Each highlighted country has 30 annual observations. Ranked coverage is 140 economies in 1995–1999, 144 in 2000–2005, and 145 from 2006 onward. Economies are drawn only where observations exist; missing ranks are not filled and series are split at gaps. Curves connect annual observations, not independently measured continuous rankings. Rounded scores can tie; the supplied published ranks are unique. The Dataverse descriptive coverage field ends at 2023, while the actual version 12.0 CSV includes 2024.

The contextual annotations draw on the World Bank's [Viet Nam 2045: Trading Up in a Changing World](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/vietnam/publication/viet-nam-2045-trading-up-in-a-changing-world) and [Viet Nam export-diversification discussion](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099032124063537994/pdf/P1770151f0870f0a21bff71b52c2fd5976f.pdf), Harvard Kennedy School's [discussion of increasingly sophisticated Chinese manufacturing](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/china-us-faces-challenges-achieving-inclusive-growth-through-manufacturing), and Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade [2024 leading exports table](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/australias-goods-and-services-by-top-25-exports-2024.pdf.pdf). These are context, not a decomposition or causal explanation of ECI changes.

Ranks and normalized scores are relative. Coverage, classification concordances, reporting differences, revisions, gross export composition, and inclusion thresholds can affect them. Export sophistication does not directly measure domestic value added or know-how in every part of the economy; assembling sophisticated products can rely on imported inputs. Australia's commodity specialization does not establish generalized economic deterioration. See Harvard's [trade-data methodology](https://atlas.hks.harvard.edu/trade-data-methodology/). No causal effect can be inferred from these trajectories alone.
The Philippines contextual annotation is supported by the Department of Trade and Industry's [discussion of its semiconductor and electronics export specialization](https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-news-archived/dti-dti-chief-pascual-encourages-us-companies-invest-semiconductor-industry). It describes export composition rather than the domestic value added of every stage of semiconductor production.
