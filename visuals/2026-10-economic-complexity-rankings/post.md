One of the more interesting consequences of China's industrialization was what it meant for Australia's economy.

Since 1995, China climbed from 42nd to 10th in economic complexity, while Australia fell from 33rd to 73rd.

These two developments were connected.

As China's manufacturing expanded, so did its demand for Australian iron ore and coal. This helped fuel Australia's mining boom, reinforcing its specialization in natural resources.

China moved into increasingly sophisticated manufacturing, while Australia's goods exports became more commodity-intensive.

In other words, the same industrial transformation that helped propel China up the rankings also contributed to the forces behind Australia's relative decline.

But China wasn't alone.

Vietnam climbed from 119th to 44th, and the Philippines from 69th to 38th. Both also overtook Australia.

Their rise reflects growing participation in electronics and manufacturing supply chains, part of a broader shift in the composition of Asian exports.

Economic complexity measures the diversity and sophistication of goods exports, not national wealth. It should also be noted that a country's ranking reflects its performance relative to other countries, not just changes in its own economic complexity.

Source: Harvard Growth Lab, 1995–2024.
