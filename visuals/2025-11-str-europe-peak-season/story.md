Europe's short-term rental markets show a shared summer peak despite differences in climate and location. The charts examine 2024 occupancy data for 25 large markets, comparing monthly patterns and the length of each market's busy season.

## Why summer demand can align

The original article proposes seven factors that could align travel demand across Europe:

- School holidays and collective workplace breaks concentrate travel opportunities from late June to early September.
- Airlines and rail operators increase summer capacity and seasonal connections.
- The “big summer holiday” remains a shared cultural expectation in many countries.
- International arrivals can sustain demand in southern destinations even when some local travelers avoid peak heat.
- Long summer daylight makes northern destinations attractive for outdoor trips and festivals.
- International arrivals to Europe tend to peak in the summer quarter.
- Seasonal hosts add short-term rental supply when demand is highest.

These are explanations presented in the [original analysis](https://github.com/mtsinganis/the-data-decoded-visualizations/blob/main/visuals/2025-11-str-europe-peak-season/index.qmd). The AirDNA occupancy charts show timing, but do not by themselves test the relative contribution of each factor. Supporting sources for the causal explanations need review before this draft is published.

## Sources and methodology

The occupancy data are attributed to AirDNA (2025) in the original charts. The Quarto analysis combines country data, compares monthly occupancy, and calculates the share of the year spent near peak occupancy. Its full processing steps and definitions remain in the [original analysis](https://github.com/mtsinganis/the-data-decoded-visualizations/blob/main/visuals/2025-11-str-europe-peak-season/index.qmd).
