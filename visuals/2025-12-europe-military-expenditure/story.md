The first chart tracks changes in European military-spending ranks from 2000 to 2024. The second compares military expenditure as a share of GDP in 2024. Its annotations discuss proximity to Russia, the war in Ukraine, and Greece–Turkey security concerns. Those are contextual interpretations; the spending data alone cannot establish why any country chose its budget.

## Sources and methodology

Both charts use the [SIPRI Military Expenditure Database 2025](https://www.sipri.org/databases/milex). SIPRI [reports Ukraine's 2024 military burden at 34% of GDP](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2025/unprecedented-rise-global-military-expenditure-european-and-middle-east-spending-surges). The [Greek Ministry of National Defence](https://www.mod.mil.gr/en/speech-minister-national-defence-nikolaos-panagiotopoulos-parliament-during-discussion/) identifies concerns about Turkey in its defence policy, but this does not measure their contribution to 2024 spending. The spending-rank chart uses current U.S. dollars. Rankings are relative: a country's position can change when another country's spending changes. The [original Quarto analysis](https://github.com/mtsinganis/the-data-decoded-visualizations/blob/main/visuals/2025-12-europe-military-expenditure/index.qmd) contains the country selection, calculations, and chart construction.
