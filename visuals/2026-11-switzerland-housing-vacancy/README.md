# Switzerland's housing vacancy rate

Run `analysis.R` from the repository root with R packages readxl, readr, ggplot2, grid, ragg, svglite, systemfonts, rsvg and png. SVG optimization also requires Python with `fonttools[woff]` and `uharfbuzz`; set `TDD_PYTHON` to that interpreter if `python` is not on PATH. The script reuses `visuals/2026-10-eu-youth-unemployment/optimize-svg-fonts.py` and the repository's fonts, theme and logo. It regenerates the CSV, PNG and SVG offline from the archived workbook.

## Source and extraction

The original [FSO table, Empty dwellings: development, 1980–2026](https://statistik.admin.ch/de/asset/36841126), published 10 September 2026, is archived as `data/fso-36841126.xlsx` ([original download](https://dam-api.bfs.admin.ch/hub/api/dam/assets/36841126/master)). The script selects the year and the national `Leerwohnungsziffer` column (column M) from sheet `T 09.03.04.01`, retaining 1984–2026 and writing `data/annual-vacancy-rate.csv`. Values are percentage units, not fractions. The workbook stores some rates at more precision than displayed: these cell values are retained without rounding; labels are rounded to two decimals. In particular 1998 is 1.8488317006757662%, displayed as 1.85%. No years are missing or imputed.

The series maximum is 1998 (1.85% displayed), and its minimum is 1989 (0.43%). The rate fell from 1.72% in 2020 to 0.93% in 2026, a 45.93% relative decrease, or 0.79 percentage points, across six successive declines. The [FSO release](https://www.bfs.admin.ch/en/newnsb/kgvqAd91MYZr) confirms the 2026 figure. The 1984–2026 period follows the [FSO's historical chart](https://statistik.admin.ch/de/asset/36836191); it is not a claim about all earlier years.

## Definitions and limitations

The census measures dwellings vacant on June 1 and offered for sale or for rental of at least three months; it is not a count of every unoccupied dwelling. Since 1994 it has also counted furnished dwellings offered for long-term rent, and classified dwellings separately as for rent or for sale. Comparisons across 1994 therefore need care. See the [FSO methodology and FAQ](https://statistik.admin.ch/de/daten-statistiken-verstehen/erhebungen/lwz).

The workbook says that an approximate prior-year housing stock was used through 2010, and that the stock from the Buildings and Dwellings Statistics has been used since 2010. Those two footnotes overlap in 2010, so the precise transition boundary is not resolved here. It also flags revisions to 2012–2015, 2017 and 2019. The FSO describes the present denominator as the previous year's register-based housing stock. These changes limit strict comparison across the full series. The workbook gives no estimate of their effect on this national series.

## Canton small multiples

`cantons.R` creates `plots/switzerland-housing-vacancy-cantons.png` and the optimized SVG, using the same R and Python font-optimizer dependencies described above. The 21.5 × 21-inch PNG is exported at 300 dpi. Each panel uses identical axes and highlights one canton in blue; the red line is the published national vacancy rate, weighted by the underlying national housing stock, not an unweighted average of canton rates. Light-grey shading shows the gap between the canton and the national rate, with exact crossings inserted between annual values for the ribbon; markers appear only at the end of the blue and red series. The panels are ordered by 2026 rate, ascending, with abbreviation as the tie-breaker. Flags were omitted to keep the 26 panels legible and the project independent of extra image assets.

The project archives the FSO's [annual canton table](https://statistik.admin.ch/en/asset/36841127) (`data/fso-cantons-36841127.xlsx`) and [five-year canton comparison table](https://statistik.admin.ch/en/asset/36841131) (`data/fso-cantons-36841131.xlsx`). The annual table provides published canton rates from 1999 through 2026, the longest complete series in these official rate tables. Earlier canton census counts are available, but were not converted into rates or spliced from other sources. The clean `data/canton-vacancy-rate.csv` has 728 observations, with year, canton, abbreviation and rate in percent; the existing national CSV supplies the Swiss series. Cell precision is retained. For 2003–2026, the script uses the most recent overlapping five-year sheet for each year, incorporating revisions; for 1999–2002 it uses the annual sheets. The tables occasionally differ when earlier canton results were later revised.

The canton workbook supplies both old and new housing-stock bases for 2010. The script selects the new Buildings and Dwellings Statistics (GWS) basis to match the published national series. The move from approximate housing-stock estimates to the GWS around 2010 means the full 1999–2026 series is not strictly methodologically uniform. Several subsequent observations were revised; the 1994 census-definition change described above predates this chart. Interpret long-run comparisons with these limitations in mind. The chart appears alongside the national series and canton rankings in the website story.
